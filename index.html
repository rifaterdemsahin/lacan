A reading site from the original teaching charts

How a subject makes meaning, proves itself, meets lack, and knots a life.

These pages unpack a set of Lacanian maps: four clinical structures, a six-step cycle, the objects each structure clings to, and the move from symptom to sinthome. The charts are the source. The pages are the explanation.

Clinical structures

Lacan does not treat these as personality types. They are positions toward the Symbolic, the Imaginary, the Real, and the desire of the Other. The charts also add a fifth row: the ethical / sinthomatic subject, who stays loyal to their own desire.

How to read this site

Start with the cycle

If you only learn one sequence, learn this: the subject produces meaning in the Symbolic, seeks proof in the Imaginary, meets trauma in the Real, answers with a symptom, repeats a formula, and may knot a sinthome.

Then compare structures

The same six steps look different in obsession, hysteria, perversion, and psychosis. That comparison is the point of the original tables.

Compare the structures →
These maps are teaching tools, not diagnostic labels. The original charts say so in a footnote on almost every image: each subject is singular; the table shows tendencies, not identities.
