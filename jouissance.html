Beyond the pleasure principle

Jouissance

Jouissance is not happiness. It is surplus enjoyment: pleasure pushed past the limit, mixed with pain, always too much. It is what the symptom secretly serves. The late chart in this archive draws it as a loop, not a prize.

The six-beat process

1

Other’s lack

The subject senses that the Other is missing something — and so are they.

2

Drive

The drive does not want a thing; it circles the hole.

3

Desire

That circling is read as a wish: I want the missing thing.

4

Jouissance

The circling itself produces surplus — attractive and destructive.

5

Lack returns

Jouissance does not close the hole; it reproduces it.

6

New desire

A new orbit begins. Continuous motion.

Drive, desire, jouissance — not synonyms

Lacan’s summary on the chart: the subject starts by focusing on the Other’s lack; drive circles that lack; desire is born in the circling; jouissance appears in the circling itself; jouissance does not seal the lack, so a new desire and a new orbit begin.

If you came here from neuroscience, read Lacan and dopamine next: dopamine is not jouissance.
