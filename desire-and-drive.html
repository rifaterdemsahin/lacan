Désir · Trieb · manque

Desire and drive

Desire is not a need, and drive is not an instinct. Need can be satisfied. Demand is spoken to the Other. Desire is what is left: a want that is structured by the Other’s want. Drive is the repetitive push around a hole, not a GPS toward an object.

Lack comes first

The subject is constituted in lack (manque). Something is missing in language, in the body, in the Other. Desire is the movement that lack makes possible. If lack were filled, desire would die — which is why fantasies of total satisfaction are both longed for and feared.

Desire is the desire of the Other

This famous sentence means at least two things on these charts:

  1. I desire what I imagine the Other desires (I want to be wanted).
  2. My desire is formatted by the Other’s signifiers — law, family, culture, a gaze.

The hysteric stays closest to this riddle. The obsessive hides their desire behind duty. The perverse subject tries to occupy the place of the object for the Other. The ethical subject chooses a desire they can be the agent of.

Drive circles; it does not arrive

Freud’s drive has source, pressure, aim, object. Lacan stresses the circuit: the aim is not to get the object but to go around it. Satisfaction is in the loop. That is why symptoms repeat, and why “just stop” is not a theory of change.
