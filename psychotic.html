Foreclosure · homemade Symbolic

The psychotic subject

For Lacan, psychosis is not “more neurosis.” It is a different knot. The Name-of-the-Father — the signifier that would bar the Other and install a shared law — is foreclosed. What neurosis represses, psychosis may meet as invasion: voices, certainty, a world that will not hold.

Meaning without a shared guarantor

The charts say: when the common Symbolic is not enough, the subject builds a private order — delusion, a system, a mystical frame. That construction is already an attempt to stabilize the Real. Proof then becomes: make this order true, for me and if possible for others.

Why the sinthome matters especially here

Late Lacan reads Joyce as a subject who did not go mad because writing functioned as a sinthome: a practice that knotted RSI without relying on a standard paternal metaphor. The teaching charts translate that idea into clinic: help the subject find their knot — music, ritual, craft, a chosen rhythm — rather than only tearing down the private order.

Stabilizing object

On the object charts, psychosis binds to a stabilizing object: sounds, writings, special routines, numbers, a faith-system. The aim named on the chart is to hold the world together. The ethical turn is not “drop the object,” but let a sinthome object — making, a practice — do the holding with more play and more shared life.
