The first Other

Over-attachment to parental figures

Parents are often the first occupants of the Big Other: law, love, gaze, the one who seems to know. This chart asks, stage by stage, why a structure leans too hard on that figure — and what happens if the loop does not turn.

Why attach so hard?

The footer of the image lists ordinary reasons: to meet needs for love, approval, safety, belonging; to lower the anxiety that comes from lack; to keep a sense of identity; to fill emptiness. None of that is childish in itself. It becomes a freeze when the parent remains the only guarantor of meaning, proof, and calm.

How the parent is used

Obsessive

Parent as law and correct judge. No meaning without their approval. Separation feels like crime.

Hysteric

Parent as the one who loves, sees, prefers. Identity leans on being the chosen one. Separation feels like erasure.

Perverse

Parent as power to use or outplay. Law is a tool. Separation feels like loss of the throne.

Psychotic

Parent as the one who holds the world. Without that figure, meaning and reality may shake.

Ethical subject

Parents can be loved without being the guarantee. Gratitude without fusion. The subject’s own desire remains the compass. Separation is possible without catastrophe.

How the chart says the bind can loosen

  • Admit lack and separation.
  • Find one’s own desire and its limits.
  • Stop idealizing the parent.
  • Take back responsibility that was parked there.
  • Look for a sinthome, not a replacement parent.
  • Build a real relation (which is not fusion).
