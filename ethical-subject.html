Sinthomatic subject · loyalty to desire

The ethical subject

The fifth row on these charts is not a fifth pathology. It is the name for a subject who accepts lack, stops using the Other as a guarantee, and takes responsibility for their unconscious desire. Lacan: do not give up on your desire. The charts: live a knot, not a hiding place.

What “ethical” means here

Not moralism. An ethic of desire: I will not outsource my being to the Other’s approval, nor cover lack by making someone else into a scene, nor freeze life into a perfect rule. I will make a form — a sinthome — that I can inhabit.

Meaning

Kept open. Produced from inside. Not a weapon and not a cage.

Proof

Less hunger to be certified. A style offered, not a verdict begged.

Trauma

Lack is admitted and turned. The wound is not denied, dumped, or worshipped.

Conditions for choosing one’s unconscious desire

Two charts spell the conditions structure by structure: what one is looking for (meaning), whom one proves oneself to, what one is trying to cover (trauma), how one lives the symptom, and whether a sinthome can hold a chosen desire.

The punchline on the compact chart: to choose one’s unconscious desire is to stay loyal to the truth of the unconscious; to make peace with lack; to build a creative and responsible life. The last cell of the ethical row says it in the first person: “The agent of my desire is me.”
