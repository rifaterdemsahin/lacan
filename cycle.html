The six-step cycle

Every chart in this archive is a variation on one sequence. A subject looks for meaning, tries to prove they exist for someone, hits lack, answers with a symptom, turns that answer into a life formula, and may finally knot a sinthome.

1

Meaning

Symbolic order. Who am I? What does this world mean?

2

Proof

Imaginary order. Confirm my value in the Other’s gaze.

3

Trauma

The Real. Lack, loss, and what cannot be said.

4

Symptom

An original, costly reply to trauma.

5

Formula

The reply becomes a repeating strategy.

6

Sinthome

A knot of the three registers; a way to live.

1. Meaning — Anlam (Symbolic)

The subject is thrown into language. Meaning is never a private invention; it is borrowed from the Other’s store of signifiers. The question at this stage is existential: Does this world mean anything, and do I?

When meaning is treated as something that must be complete, the subject panics at ambiguity. The ethical move, on the charts, is to keep meaning open-ended — to leave a place for the unknown.

2. Proof — İspat (Imaginary)

Proof is the demand to be seen. In the Imaginary, the subject wants an image that would finally say: you count. This is where the gaze, comparison, performance, and the hunger for recognition live.

The charts treat proof as a trap if it depends only on the Other’s approval. The way through is to return to one’s own measure, and to share a style rather than beg for a stamp.

3. Trauma — Travma (Real)

Trauma here is not only an event. It is the encounter with lack: something missing in the Other, in language, in the body. The Real is what does not fit the story. Loss, castration, the fact that there is no complete answer.

Structures differ by how they meet this: the obsessive intellectualizes or delays; the hysteric dramatizes and questions; the perverse subject disavows; the psychotic subject may be flooded.

4. Symptom — Semptom

A symptom is already a solution — a costly one. It is how jouissance is bound. Rituals, questions, staging, delusions: each is a way not to fall apart. Lacan reads the symptom as a message and as a mode of enjoyment, not as noise to delete.

5. Formula — Formül

If the symptom works well enough, it becomes a formula: a personal algorithm. “If I control everything, I am safe.” “If I keep asking, desire stays alive.” “If I stage the scene, I hold the jouissance.” Life starts to run on that function.

A frozen formula makes the subject mechanical. A living formula can be revised, seasonal, shared.

6. Sinthome

The sinthome is not the absence of a symptom. It is the symptom reworked as a knot: a singular practice (writing, craft, a style of love, a discipline) that holds Imaginary, Symbolic, and Real together. The subject lives with lack instead of against it.

Two endings of the same cycle

Unresolved

Meaning hardens or collapses. Proof depends on others. Trauma is denied or somatized. The symptom is only a shield. The formula becomes a cage. The inner world fragments or freezes.

Worked through toward a sinthome

Meaning stays open. Value is authored. Lack is admitted and turned. The symptom is read. A formula is chosen ethically. A knot is made: creative, limited, livable.

A later family of charts reverses the first two steps after trauma: Trauma → Meaning → Proof → Symptom → Existence. That is the same logic seen from the Real first. See the UML / process pages.
