Disavowal · staging · fetish

The perverse subject

In Lacanian clinic, perversion is not a moral insult. It is a position: the subject disavows lack and tries to make themselves the instrument of the Other’s jouissance. Law is used, staged, bent. The scene replaces the hole.

What the charts emphasize

Meaning is produced by rewriting the law for one’s own use and by making others into functions in a scene. Proof is power: I can move the Other. Trauma — the fact of lack — is denied or pushed onto someone else. The symptom is fetish, staging, control dressed as play. The formula is a script that must be recast.

Disavowal in one sentence

Freud’s fetishist “knows very well, but even so.” The charts write this as: lack is seen and immediately covered by an object, a role, a technology, a rule used as a whip or a toy. The fetish object is not random; it is the plug for castration.

Toward an ethical use of the same gifts

The perverse subject’s resources — scene-making, intensity, reading the Other — are not thrown away on the ethical row. They are turned: meaning is internalized rather than used on others; desire is stated rather than staged as a trap; lack is admitted; jouissance is put into making rather than into holding someone in a scene.

The unresolved arrow on these charts names real costs: isolation, violence, freeze, collapse of trust. The map is ethical because it shows those costs, not because it scolds.
