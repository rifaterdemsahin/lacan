The Big Other

The Big Other is not a person. It is the place of language, law, and presumed knowledge: the treasury of signifiers we are born into. Parents, institutions, God, “what one does,” the gaze of a culture — all can occupy that place. The subject is spoken before they speak.

Big Other vs little other

other (imaginary)

The counterpart: rival, mirror, peer, the one I compare my image with. Proof lives here.

Other (symbolic)

The locus of law and speech. Meaning lives here. So does the question: what does the Other want of me?

Name-of-the-Father

The paternal metaphor is a function, not a dad. It substitutes a Name for the mother’s desire, installing a limit. When that signifier is foreclosed, the psychotic subject lacks a shared guarantee and may meet the Real without a screen. When it is in place, neurosis can repress, doubt, and question.

How each structure uses the Other

  • Obsessive: hides from the Other’s demand by serving an impersonal law, or by delaying so the demand never quite arrives.
  • Hysteric: addresses the Other as the supposed subject of knowledge: tell me what I am.
  • Perverse: makes themselves the object that would complete the Other, while pulling the strings of the scene.
  • Psychotic: the Other may be too real — persecutory, invasive — or replaced by a homemade Other.
